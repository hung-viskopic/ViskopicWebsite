01 — The Problem

Detection is a losing race. Provenance isn't.

Every AI-detection classifier is trained against a model of AI writing that is already out of date by the time it ships.1 As generation models improve, the statistical fingerprints these tools rely on shrink, and false positives fall disproportionately on exactly the writers institutions are trying to protect: non-native speakers, neurodivergent students, and anyone whose natural style resembles what a model has learned to imitate.2

Meanwhile, the actual question an educator, editor, or hiring manager wants answered is rarely "does this look machine-generated?" It's "can this person show me how they got here?" That's an authorship question, not a detection question, and it needs a different kind of infrastructure: one built at the point of writing, not reconstructed forensically after the fact.

  • 1. Detector accuracy degrades measurably against newer generations of language models, see ongoing evaluations from AI safety and NLP research groups.
  • 2. Elevated false-positive rates for non-native English writers have been documented in independent evaluations of commercial AI-text classifiers.
02 — Our Approach

Infrastructure, not inference.

Method A

Process capture

Instead of analysing finished text, we instrument the writing process itself: drafting patterns, revision history, timing, to build a verifiable record of how a piece of writing came to exist.

Method B

Stylometric baselines

We build a longitudinal model of an individual's own writing style, so authorship claims are checked against that person's history, not a generic "AI vs. human" boundary that shifts under every model release.

Method C

Portable credentials

Authorship evidence is issued as a portable, verifiable record the writer controls: usable across institutions, platforms and employers, rather than locked inside one vendor's dashboard.
